# AdRoute Fleet — Deep-Research Market Analysis

**Prepared:** August 1, 2026
**Scope:** US mobile-billboard-truck marketplace opportunity; launch region Georgia + Florida (Atlanta → Orlando, Tampa, Jacksonville, Miami-Dade mainland; excluding Miami Beach and Key West)
**Method:** ~200 web searches + page analysis across OAAA releases, municipal codes, operator sites, funding databases, and trade press, executed by five parallel research workstreams. Every key finding carries a confidence tag **[High/Medium/Low]** based on source quality and corroboration. Numbers that could not be verified from at least two independent sources are explicitly flagged **⚠ SINGLE-SOURCE** or **⚠ UNVERIFIED**. A methodology note and known research gaps appear at the end.

---

## Executive Summary

**The whitespace is real, but the pond is smaller than the pitch decks claim.** No company today offers self-serve online booking of mobile billboard trucks for advertisers under $25K — every truck operator and broker checked (10+, including Blue Line Media, Can't Miss US, Bulldog, Billboard Express) transacts by phone/email quote, and AdQuick, the closest analog, gates its self-serve product at a $25,000 minimum and routes truck inventory through quote-driven workflows [High on the quote-only landscape; the AdQuick Go $25K minimum is ⚠ SINGLE-SOURCE from AdQuick's own marketplace page and must be re-verified]. Blip has proven the sub-$1K self-serve OOH buyer exists — but only for fixed digital boards, with zero truck supply [High].

**Correct market frame:** The US OOH industry hit a record **$9.46B in 2025 (+3.6%)**, with DOOH at 36.3% growing 10.5% — your baseline numbers are right [High, OAAA]. But the widely-quoted "$11.3B North America mobile billboard market" (market.us) is not credible: it exceeds all US digital OOH (~$3.4B) by 3x through scope inflation (buses, transit, hardware sales). The defensible US dedicated-ad-truck segment is **~$300–900M/yr, central estimate ~$500M** (bottom-up: ~2,000–4,000 trucks × $110–290K/truck/yr) [Medium — analyst construction, no census exists]. The GA+FL five-metro serviceable market is **~$25–60M/yr, central ~$40–50M** [Low-Medium].

**Supply:** GA/FL supply is highly fragmented — an estimated **65–115 dedicated trucks (midpoint ~85–90)**, mostly 1–4 truck owner-operators; the largest verified in-region fleet is Mobile Billboard Miami at 10+ trucks. Marketplace willingness is demonstrably high (10+ regional operators already list on DOmedia; AdQuick claims vetted truck operators in every major DMA) [Medium].

**Demand:** Local advertisers are structurally the OOH majority (65% of OOH sales are local, OAAA) [High], and published day rates ($800–$2,400 static, $1,200–$3,600/4,000 LED) mean nearly every single-truck campaign under 3 weeks is a sub-$25K transaction. 2026 is a demand tailwind: a projected record $11.6B political cycle with marquee GA governor and FL governor + Senate races [High].

**Regulatory:** Key West's ban is confirmed in code (Sec. 70-804); Miami Beach's restrictions are real; Miami-Dade mainland targets *parked* advertising vehicles, leaving moving trucks operable; **but Jacksonville's code contains explicitly hostile mobile-billboard language that must be resolved before it's counted as a launch market, and Atlanta/Savannah/Orlando/Tampa vehicle-sign clauses could not be definitively verified** [Medium — flagged as the top diligence item].

**Verdict: conditional GO** — as a wedge, not an endgame. The niche is winnable (fragmented supply, no self-serve incumbent, weak channel competition beyond SEO brokers), but a ~$40–50M regional SAM at a 15–20% take rate caps the GA/FL prize at roughly **$6–10M annual revenue at full saturation** — a strong beachhead only if the platform expands formats (trailers, boats, aerial, street teams) and geography quickly. Full recommendation, the three assumptions to validate with operators, and an investor-ready paragraph are at the end.

**Why market.us's $11.3B is wrong [High confidence in the rejection]:**
1. **Sanity anchor:** total US *digital* OOH = 36.3% × $9.46B ≈ **$3.43B** (OAAA). A "mobile digital billboard" segment can't be 3x the entire digital OOH category it sits inside. Mobile billboards aren't even a named OAAA revenue format.
2. **Scope inflation:** the definition folds in buses/transit and, in practice, all vehicle-borne DOOH.
3. **Value-chain double-counting:** these reports count truck/display *manufacturing* (LED trucks sell for $50K–$150K, per [Can't Miss US truck sales](https://cantmiss.us/truck-sales/)) plus media plus operations; OAAA counts ad revenue only.
4. **Irreproducibility:** the largest verifiable US fleet claim is Bulldog's ~170 vehicles; even 4,000 trucks at an aggressive $290K/yr each is <$1.2B.

**Defensible bottom-up estimate [Medium — analyst construction; ⚠ no published truck census exists, flagged accordingly]:**
- Plausible national population of dedicated ad trucks: **2,000–4,000** (anchors: Bulldog ~170 claimed "largest fleet in North America" [Medium]; a long tail of 1–10-truck operators across 100+ metros; Geopath/OAAA publish no census).
- Utilization 35–55% of ~260 sellable days = 90–145 booked days/truck/yr; blended realized rate $1,200–$2,000/day → **$110K–$290K revenue/truck/yr**.
- **Implied US segment: $220M–$900M; central ≈ $450–550M (~5–6% of US OOH).**
- Triangulation: the most conservative research figure (global $1.73B, 2025) at a typical 40–50% US share implies $700–870M — same order of magnitude [Medium].

## 2. Competitor Analysis

### The critical question first

> **Does anyone already own self-serve mobile-billboard booking under $25K? No.** [High]

Evidence: every mobile-billboard supplier and broker checked (~10 vendors) transacts via quote form/phone/email — no cart, no online payment, no live availability calendar anywhere. AdQuick is the only genuine multi-operator truck marketplace, and it gates self-serve at $25K and routes trucks through "instant quote" + campaign-manager workflows. True sub-$25K self-serve OOH exists only for **fixed** digital billboards (Blip, from $10–50/day). Two caveats: AdQuick could drop its minimum, and Blip could point its playbook at trucks — see Risks.

### 2.1 AdQuick — closest threat, structurally pulled upmarket

- **Model:** OOH marketplace + SaaS; 1,800+ media owners, 1.6M+ placements ([adquick.com/marketplace](https://www.adquick.com/marketplace)) [High]. Has a **dedicated mobile-billboard product** — "LED truck ads in 300+ cities," aggregating "dozens of vetted operators," $800–$3,600/day, ~48-hr LED launch ([adquick.com/mobile-billboard-advertising](https://www.adquick.com/mobile-billboard-advertising)) [Medium — snippet-derived].
- **Fees:** take rate **not published**; monetizes the media-owner side, consistent with OOH's traditional ~15–16.7% commission norm [Low — inference, flagged]. Minimums: $25K marketplace [High]; AdQuick Go self-serve also $25K [⚠ SINGLE-SOURCE].
- **Funding/ownership:** ~$30.4M raised; **OUTFRONT Media took a corporate minority stake Feb 25, 2026 — up to $20M at milestones, plus an exclusive 3-year license of AdQuick's "OOH sales cloud"** ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/outfront-media-and-adquick-form-exclusive-commercial-partnership-and-strategic-equity-investment-to-accelerate-how-irl-media-campaigns-are-built-measured-and-executed-302697491.html)) [High].
- **Revenue:** $4.2M (2025), 38 employees, headcount −30% YoY per GetLatka [⚠ Low — single aggregator, frequently stale].
- **Strengths:** brand ("book billboards online"), measurement stack, real truck supply relationships, OUTFRONT capital.
- **Gap for AdRoute:** the $25K floor excludes the SMB segment outright; truck booking isn't instant checkout; the OUTFRONT alignment points AdQuick at premium *fixed* inventory and national brands, away from $3K truck campaigns in Marietta.

### 2.2 Blip — the UX benchmark and the most dangerous fast-follower

- Self-serve auction marketplace, **fixed digital billboards only**: 5,000+ boards, 8-second "blips," budgets from $10–50/day, no contracts ([blipbillboards.com](https://www.blipbillboards.com/)) [High]. **Zero mobile-truck inventory** [High].
- Independent (Orem, UT, founded 2015); $16.3M raised (Decathlon Capital, others) [Medium]. Revenue estimates conflict: $22.6M vs $8.2M across aggregators [⚠ Low — both single-source; flag].
- **Acquired Sign Dash (billboard operations software — proposals, contracts, work orders) Dec 3, 2025** ([Digital Signage Pulse](https://digitalsignagepulse.com/news/self-serve-ooh-ad-platform-blip-acquires-billboard-operations-software-firm-sign-dash/)) [High] — vertical integration into operator tooling, exactly the lock-in play AdRoute should copy for truck operators.
- **Gap:** format-locked; its RTB/slot model doesn't map to day-rate, route-based, single-advertiser trucks. But it has the SMB funnel, checkout UX, and operator-tools playbook — if AdRoute proves the category, Blip is the most credible entrant behind it.

### 2.3 Fliphound — cautionary tale, not competitor
Alive but tiny: **3 employees** (Tracxn, Apr 2026), no funding ever raised, Wichita-based, fixed digital boards only [Medium]. Lesson: self-serve DOOH without capital or supply density stalls.

### 2.4 OneScreen.ai — turned around, not dead (correcting the record)
The 2023 "winding down" reports **could not be confirmed** [⚠ UNVERIFIED]. What's documented: a 2023 investor-led restructuring (Asymmetric Capital installed CEO Alex Ewing; opex cut 50%; revenue tripled), a **$4.7M raise in June 2024** ([FinSMEs](https://www.finsmes.com/2024/06/onescreen-ai-raises-4-7m-in-funding.html)), claimed profitability, and a "68% Q1 surge" announcement in 2026 [Medium-High]. Model: concierge/managed OOH for B2B/startup brands (Ramp, Karat), Northeast bias. Not fighting for $5K local campaigns; no self-serve truck checkout [Medium].

### 2.5 DOmedia / BillboardsIn — infrastructure, not a rival for the SMB buyer
Agency-side supplier database + RFP software; 15,000+ assets on BillboardsIn; company-stated $10B planned / ~$2B contracted through platform in 2024; still independent [Medium]. Mobile-billboard vendors are listed (Blue Line has a [vendor profile](https://www.domedia.com/d/public/company/16344)) but transactions are RFP/quote, not checkout [High]. **Strategic significance for AdRoute: DOmedia listings are a pre-qualified operator recruiting list — 10+ GA/FL-relevant truck operators already maintain profiles there** (Section 3).

### 2.6 Vistar Media — confirmed T-Mobile acquisition; irrelevant near-term
Announced Jan 13, 2025 at $600M; **closed Feb 3, 2025; actual cash $621M** per T-Mobile's 10-Q ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1283699/000128369925000154/tmus-20250930.htm), [T-Mobile](https://www.t-mobile.com/news/business/t-mobile-to-acquire-vistarmedia-dooh-advertising)) [High]. Programmatic SSP/DSP for digital screens — touches LED trucks only if operators sell impressions programmatically. Signal value: programmatic rails for LED trucks exist for a future AdRoute integration (Movia already went programmatic via Perion/Hivestack) [Medium-High].

## 4. Demand Side

**Who buys [Medium — operator marketing + case studies, consistent across sources]:**
- Verticals repeatedly cited by operators ([Can't Miss](https://cantmiss.us/blogs/mobile-billboard-advertising-benefit-industries/), [Movia](https://moviamedia.com/moving-billboard-blog/types-of-mobile-billboards/)): retail, restaurants/QSR, real estate/developments, automotive, entertainment/events, healthcare, politics, education, finance, fitness.
- **Conventions/trade shows are a confirmed, productized niche:** Mobile Billboard Miami runs a dedicated [convention-show service](https://www.mobilebillboardmiami.com/convention-show) (booth numbers, QR codes, routes around hotel blocks and venue exits); ILUM names the Orange County Convention Center as a core Orlando targeting zone [High that the product exists].
- Political: candidate awareness/event-day usage documented (Movia, Blue Line case studies); Rolling Box runs Atlanta election-truck landing pages [Medium].
- Real estate: OUTFRONT/Blue Line case study (Spectrum Capital, New Orleans) reported a 319.67% lift over mobile secondary-action benchmarks [Medium — vendor case study].
- ⚠ **Gaps flagged honestly:** no retrieved case studies for Atlanta game-day/SEC Championship, Art Basel/F1/Ultra activations, college/greek life, or medspa — plausible but unevidenced this pass.

**Budgets and the sub-$25K share:**
- No published data on "share of purchases under $25K" exists [⚠ Gap]. But the arithmetic is decisive: at consensus rates, a 1-day activation runs $1.5–4K, a week $7–15K, and only a 3+ week LED campaign crosses $25K. **Structurally, nearly all single-truck campaigns shorter than ~3 weeks are sub-$25K transactions** [Medium — inference from verified rate cards, not survey data].
- Local advertisers = **65% of OOH sales** (OAAA 2025 Facts & Figures) [High]. ## 7. Top 5 Risks

**1. Disintermediation (highest probability).** Advertiser meets operator on campaign #1, books campaign #2 by phone. Local, repeat, relationship-driven — the classic leaky-marketplace category. *Mitigation:* be the system of record, not the introduction — escrow + GPS proof-of-play + auto-generated recap reports advertisers show their bosses; rebooking discounts funded by take-rate step-downs for loyal pairs; operator-side tooling (scheduling, invoicing, routing — the Blip/Sign Dash playbook) so leaving the platform means losing the back office.

**2. Thin, overlapping supply.** ~85–90 trucks region-wide, and four different "largest fleet in America" claims recycling the same owner-operators [Medium]. Availability failures on peak dates (conventions, election weeks) would kill early NPS. *Mitigation:* sign the 3 anchor fleets first (Mobile Billboard Miami, ILUM, LED Truck Media's FL trucks); guaranteed-fulfillment SLA backed by a standby broker relationship; note that operators sell trucks too — supply is manufacturable, and a financing partnership with a truck builder (Legion, Can't Miss) converts demand signal into new fleet.

**3. AdQuick drops its minimum / Blip enters trucks.** AdQuick already has truck supply relationships and a self-serve product one config change away from $5K minimums; Blip has the SMB funnel and just bought operator-tools (Sign Dash, Dec 2025) [High on capabilities]. *Mitigation:* speed and locality — win GA/FL operator exclusivity on *availability data* (calendars), which neither has; AdQuick's OUTFRONT alignment (Feb 2026) is a structural distraction pulling it toward premium fixed inventory; being mobile-billboard-native (route planning, event calendars, per-day ops) is a real product moat against generalists.

**4. Regulatory whiplash in launch metros.** Jacksonville's code contains explicitly hostile mobile-billboard language [Medium]; Atlanta/Orlando/Tampa/Savannah vehicle-sign clauses are unresolved; one aggressive city attorney or a copycat of California's AB 1253 changes the map. *Mitigation:* resolve all six code questions with local counsel *before* market-entry sequencing (may demote Jacksonville); build the compliance engine as a product feature (route rules, parking rules, brightness/dimming, per-city playbooks) — it becomes a moat and an operator-recruitment hook; keep format diversity (static + LED + trailers) since restrictions usually bite LED/parked configurations first.

**5. The SAM is genuinely small.** $40–60M regional, ~$300–900M national [Medium] — at a 20% terminal take and heroic 25% regional share, GA/FL yields only ~$2–3M revenue. This is a wedge economics problem, not a failure mode per se. *Mitigation:* the roadmap (boats, aerial banners, LED trailers, street teams) isn't optional garnish — it's the TAM; Ballyhoo-style boat media and event street teams share the same buyer and the same booking rails; geographic expansion (TX, Carolinas, Nashville) should begin the moment the GA/FL playbook repeats. Secondary risk inside this one: sub-scale demand liquidity means auction dynamics won't work early — launch as "instant book at posted prices," not bidding.

*(Watch-list, below top-5: SEO-incumbent CAC pressure — Blue Line/LED Truck Media/Can't Miss own the search terms AdRoute needs, so paid acquisition against their content moats will be expensive; and political-cycle demand concentration — a 2026 revenue bump that masks weak underlying SMB repeat rates.)*

## Go / No-Go Recommendation

**GO — conditional, staged, and honestly sized.** The core thesis survives contact with the evidence: (1) the sub-$25K self-serve mobile-billboard buyer is genuinely unserved [High]; (2) supply is fragmented, broker-tolerant, and already semi-commoditized on price — ideal marketplace conditions [Medium-High]; (3) 2026's record political cycle and Florida's event economy give the launch window unusual demand tailwinds [High]. But the segment is a ~$40–60M regional / ~$500M national niche, not the $11B fantasy — so fund it as a **wedge**: win GA/FL mobile trucks, then expand formats and states on the same rails. Three conditions before full commitment: **(a)** legal verification of Atlanta, Orlando, Tampa, and especially Jacksonville vehicle-sign codes (one week of local counsel; may re-sequence the launch map); **(b)** manual verification of AdQuick Go's $25K minimum (the single most load-bearing competitive fact in this report, currently single-source); **(c)** signed LOIs from at least two anchor fleets (start with Mobile Billboard Miami and ILUM) before writing marketplace code.

## The 3 Assumptions Most Worth Validating in Real Operator Conversations

1. **Idle capacity + commission tolerance:** Do GA/FL operators actually run 40–60% unsold days, and will they accept 15% commission with platform-controlled payment/escrow — or do brokers already fill their calendars at better terms? (The entire GMV model rests on this.)
2. **True sub-$25K demand volume:** How many inbound campaign requests per month does a typical operator/broker see per metro, at what average size, and what share are the short-cycle, owner-direct buys the platform is built for? (No published data exists — operator order books are the only source of truth.)
3. **Proof-of-play as the wedge:** Will advertisers pay (and rebook) for GPS-verified routes and auto-generated reporting — i.e., is verification the actual pain point that keeps the transaction on-platform, or is price? (This determines whether the disintermediation mitigation works at a 15–20% take.)

## Investor-Ready Market Summary

US out-of-home advertising just posted its 20th consecutive growth quarter — a record $9.46B in 2025, with digital growing 3× the industry rate and local advertisers making up 65% of every OOH dollar (OAAA). Yet the fastest-deploying format in the category, mobile billboard trucks — an estimated ~$500M national segment growing on LED conversion — still transacts like it's 1995: phone calls, email quotes, and zero price transparency, brokered by middlemen who own the Google results but not the booking. Nobody serves the sub-$25K local advertiser: AdQuick's self-serve starts at $25,000, and Blip's proven $20-a-day self-serve engine covers only fixed billboards. AdRoute Fleet opens this segment with the first instant-book marketplace for billboard trucks — quotes, contracts, payment, GPS proof-of-play, and reporting in one flow — launching into Georgia and Florida's five major metros (~$710M in annual OOH spend, ~$40–60M serviceable for trucks), where supply is fragmented across ~90 owner-operator trucks with no consolidator, demand is amplified by a record $11.6B 2026 political cycle and America's densest convention-and-events calendar, and an AI-native team of five can run acquisition, onboarding, and verification at a cost structure no phone-based broker can match.
